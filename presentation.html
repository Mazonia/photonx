SLIDE 02 / 07 • THE PROBLEM ASTRONOMICAL CHALLENGE

The Problem: Why Is Planet X Still Missing?

The physics of extreme optical dimness versus the challenge of massive raw spectral datasets.

❄️ 1. Optical Illumination Collapse (1/r⁴)

  • Reflected sunlight drops off as distance squared out, and distance squared back (Flux ∝ 1/r⁴).
  • At 400 AU (the hypothesized orbit of Planet X), sunlight is 25 billion times dimmer than on Earth.
  • A Neptune-mass planet has optical magnitude V > 23.5—pitch black in visible telescopes like Hubble or Gaia.
  • Thermal Glow: Even at 35 Kelvin, these bodies radiate internal heat in the near-infrared (1–5 µm).

📊 2. The Infrared Data Flood Bottleneck

  • NASA's SPHEREx mission is surveying the entire celestial sphere every 6 months across 102 spectral bands.
  • This produces millions of complex, multi-extension FITS image files locked in technical archives.
  • Astronomers and citizen scientists lack fast, visual tools to compare epochs and spot shifting objects.
  • Without interactive temporal differencing, slow-moving outer worlds remain buried in raw data.
Speaker Note: "Why haven't we found Planet X yet? Because out at 400 AU, reflected sunlight is 25 billion times fainter than on Earth. Optical surveys simply cannot see it. But in the thermal infrared, these cold bodies glow. NASA's SPHEREx mission captures this exact light across 102 bands. However, that creates a new problem: billions of raw spectral pixels locked in complex FITS files that are nearly impossible for humans to visually explore."

SLIDE 04 / 07 • DETECTION PIPELINE IMAGE DIFFERENCING ENGINE

How It Works: Automated Mover Detection

Sub-pixel image differencing that cancels fixed background stars to isolate moving centroids.

Figure 2: Image Difference Engine — Subtraction residuals and automated mover candidate list

⚡ 3-Step Differencing Pipeline

  • 1. Align & Normalize Flux: Aligns WCS celestial geometry and matches Point-Spread Functions (PSFs) between Epoch A and Epoch B (separated by 6 months).
  • 2. Subtract Matrices (ΔI = I_B − I_A): Fixed background stars cancel out to zero. A moving object leaves a distinct dipole: negative residual at Epoch A, positive at Epoch B.
  • 3. Threshold (> 3σ) & Centroid Vector: Connected-component clustering isolates significant peaks, calculates exact sub-pixel motion in arcseconds, and logs candidates.
Speaker Note: "Under the hood, PhotonX runs a mathematical image subtraction pipeline. When comparing two survey epochs, the engine normalizes the fields and subtracts Epoch A from Epoch B. Stationary stars cancel out completely. Moving objects leave a distinct dipole signature. Our algorithm flags all peaks exceeding three sigma, computes their positional shift in arcseconds, and ranks them for follow-up."
